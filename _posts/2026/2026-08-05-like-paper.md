What screens do and how we relate to them is changing and when I think of to guide us I keep landing on paper.

A blank sheet of paper can be almost anything. It is so easy to pick up and do something with it. At the same time it's an incredibly versatile canvas for expression, a tool for communication and even a medium for preserving thought over time. Paper combines accessibility and versatility effortlessly. And it's cheap enough to crumple up and forget.

Screen-based devices have approached this ideal in theory, but the cost of creating and changing software has kept authorship scarce and creators exclusive. Have anything appear a screen by your making felt special. Now, an idea and the will to manifest is enough. Minds blown.

## The Truly Personal Computer

This new idea of a personal computer isn't just about everyone being able to use one. It's that everyone can shape the entire experience around their own needs. The software user relationship becomes one to one.

Turns out the distance between that potential and reality was mostly cost. Now nothing needs justifying. The only measure is whether it matters to you. You build a tool for one task, use it for a minute or a lifetime, and let it go when it stops being useful. The crumple up and forget era of Software. It's liberating.

## Overcoming Digital Dopamine

It could feel counter intuitive but my hope is that same abundance might also help resolve our crisis of digital over stimulation. When screens become everything all at once, we might just grow tired of that rush of flashing pixels and opening up space to turn inward, becoming more aware of ourselves, the world and people around us. 

Screens turn into nearly invisible enablers for doing things when we actually need them, rather than toys we endlessly fidget with and just can't seem to let go of. In that world, screens become just paper.
