If you love scribbling on paper as I do, you know that using a pen is about so much more than inputting text. As we are moving beyond the keyboard for input, let’s not forget about that joy. 

Here's a little prototype exploring and reflecting on some of the possibilities.

## Obviously versatile

A crossed-out sentence means "not this". A question mark asks for an explanation. A circle focuses the conversation. A small sketch can express something that is awkward or hard to describe. Arrows, hearts, stars… it's not a rigid system. Your markup can be as varied as the content it annotates. And there's the fun in it.

![Markup used to inform chat responses](/assets/images/2026/2026-09-20-pencil-and-text-markup.png) 

How much of a chat flow could be driven by just these quick cues and marks? That's something I was curious to explore in this little prototype.

## No submit button

One of the greatest delights of scribbling is flow. A scribble already has a natural ending: the end of a line, the punctuation ending a sentence or simply lifting the pencil and pausing. No submit button is needed when writing on a page. Removing the button felt like a refreshing unlock in the prototype. Dissolving the back-and-forth into one continuous motion.

![Markup examples integrated with text](/assets/images/2026/2026-09-20-pencil-and-text-photo.jpeg)

## You are what's unique

There's one more unexpected aspect this exploration made me think about. In a world where everything can feel the same, your imperfect scribble as input might make a real difference. 

Seeding that bit of individual noise might lead to results that become harder to reproduce or generate than perfectly copied and pasted strings. Your hand becomes part of the input, and the input becomes yours.

This is just a tiny experiment about where pencil input might take us. But the real point might be simply that it's up to us to create the experiences we want to use. Notice where the joy is, and build it in. Efficiency isn't everything, and it isn't exclusive. Experiences can also be more accessible, expressive and simply fun at the same time.
